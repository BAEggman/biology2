A grassy campsite on a calm day. Everything is seen from the front with its front wall cut away like a dollhouse, so we see inside.

FAR LEFT EDGE: only the right part of a round dome tent of cream canvas with one red stripe; at its cut edge we can see its canvas is TWO layers with a gap between them. Its OUTER layer continues seamlessly into the roof of the next building.

LEFT HALF: a LONG, LOW TENT BARRACKS of khaki canvas with wooden poles and guy ropes. Where its walls are cut we can see the canvas is only ONE single layer.
1. The LEFT part of the barracks roof (near the dome tent) is studded with many purple pairs; each pair is a small purple lump sitting on a big purple lump. From each pair a chain of colorful beads goes down through a small hole in the roof INTO the barracks. On the roof, a small guide person holds the RED first beads at the front end of one new bead chain and leads that purple pair to an empty roof hole.
2. Inside this left part: the bead chains fold up into neat round balls; a worker sticks little lollipops into one folded ball; another worker sews a new patch of khaki canvas onto the barracks wall. One worker has a small iron anchor hanging from the end of his belt.
3. The RIGHT part of the barracks has NO purple pairs on its roof. Inside it: a worker pours yellow oil from a big glass jar marked with a golden emblem of joined rings; a worker scrubs a bottle with a skull label until the skull fades away; a tall metal locker stands with its door half open and grey gravel spilling out.
4. At the barracks' right end there is an open exit; small handcarts with round canvas covers, loaded with boxes, roll out of it toward the right.

MIDDLE: a building with TWO pointed gable roofs joined side by side, front wall cut away. Inside, a stack of flat curved trays, like stacked pancakes, rests on rails, and the trays slide from the left gable toward the right gable. The left gable faces the barracks and takes in the handcarts; the right gable sends things out.
5. At the right gable, a worker presses a plain RED stamp onto a lollipop stuck on some boxes, and those stamped boxes are loaded into a handcart covered by a cage of triangle lattice, which rolls away downward to the bottom right. Other boxes go into plain round-covered handcarts heading to the right.
6. One handcart with a big LEFT-pointing arrow painted on its side rolls back from the gabled building toward the barracks, carrying the worker with the anchor on his belt.

RIGHT: the edge of the campsite is a long wall of cream canvas (one single layer) along a calm blue lake. Plain round-covered handcarts arrive at this wall; where one handcart touches the wall, its round canvas cover melts into the wall canvas and opens to the outside, and small glass vials tumble out into the lake.
