Draw another picture in exactly the same style - warm cream paper, thin hand-inked storybook outlines, soft flat watercolour washes, on bare cream ground, no text of any kind. Square image. Keep it simple and uncluttered.

WHAT TO DRAW - three pipes meeting at one corner, seen straight on.

A THICK pipe comes down from the top left and ends in a round ball of tightly coiled tubing in the middle of the picture. A THINNER pipe leaves that ball and goes up to the top right. The thick pipe is plainly about twice as wide as the thin one.

A SQUARE-SIDED DUCT comes in from the right and bends so that one flat side of it presses against the THICK pipe, just above the coiled ball. The two touch along a short stretch. This touching stretch is the corner the picture is about.

At that corner, three things and only three things:

1) ON THE THICK PIPE'S WALL, exactly where it touches the duct, the wall swells into a POUCH packed full of small round grains, drawn so the grains are clearly visible through the pouch wall. A small curved SICKLE blade sticks out of the pouch's open mouth. The sickle has just cut a long slack ROPE that lies across the front of the picture: to the left of the cut the rope is loose and sagging, and to the right of the cut the same rope is pulled bowstring-tight between two hooks.

2) ON THE FLAT SIDE OF THE DUCT, exactly along the touching stretch and nowhere else, the duct's inner wall is crowded with short stiff BRISTLES packed tightly together. Everywhere else the duct's inner wall is completely smooth and bare. A CORD is tied round the thick pipe just upstream of the corner and is pulled tight, so the thick pipe is visibly pinched narrower at that one spot.

3) IN THE WEDGE OF EMPTY SPACE between the thick pipe, the thin pipe and the duct, a woven MESH completely fills the gap, like a net stuffed into the corner, touching all three pipes.

Nothing else is in the picture. No people, no hands, no faces.

WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN: the two pipes drawn the same width - the incoming one from the top left must be plainly thicker; the square duct drawn round like the pipes; bristles appearing anywhere on the duct except along the short stretch where it touches the thick pipe; the pouch of grains placed on the duct or on the thin pipe instead of on the thick pipe; the grains left out so the pouch reads as empty; the rope drawn slack along its whole length or taut along its whole length - it must be slack on one side of the cut and tight on the other; the sickle drawn as scissors, a knife or shears; the cord placed downstream of the corner, or the thick pipe left unpinched; the mesh drawn as a flat patch on a pipe instead of filling the wedge of space between all three; a fourth pipe; any writing, letters, numbers, labels or arrows appearing anywhere.
