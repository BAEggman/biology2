the scene

A plain workshop bay seen from inside. The left quarter of the picture is a cutaway showing the outside face of the bay's wall. Plain pale walls, a plain concrete floor, one pale green air duct running across the ceiling. Nothing decorative.

what is in it, listed from left to right

1. far left, mounted flat on the outside face of the wall. One plain grey metal mounting plate, rectangular, with four round bolt holes — one near each corner. Every hole is empty. There is no bolt, no screw, no fitting and nothing at all attached to this plate. It is completely bare.

2. middle left, inside the bay. One older man in a teal work jacket and grey trousers, walking towards the right. In his left hand he holds one small flat grey metal tray out in front of his waist, well clear of his body and clear of his sleeve, tilted a little towards the viewer so that the whole top face of it can be seen.

3. that tray holds exactly four round grey river pebbles and nothing else. The pebbles sit in four shallow round dents in the tray, set out as a square — two dents along the near edge and two dents along the far edge. That is the whole of it: there is no fifth dent, no third dent in either row and no third row. The tray is small — only wide enough for those four dents, and no part of it is empty or hidden. The pebbles are smooth and rounded like stones from a stream, all four the same size, filling their dents.

4. the same man's right hand rests on the shoulder of a second, younger man and pushes him forward to the right. It is plain that the older man is bringing the younger one along.

5. right of the two men. The younger man, in a grey shirt, leans forward and presses one wooden-handled rubber stamp down onto the flat head of a machine arm. The machine arm is a jointed grey metal arm rising from a bolted base on the floor, and its head is a flat square plate. The stamp is touching that plate.

- count the pebbles: there are four, no more and no fewer, and each one sits in its own dent
- the tray is a four-dent tray, not a six-cup egg carton and not a crate
- the tray is held out clear of the man's body so that nothing overlaps or hides any dent
