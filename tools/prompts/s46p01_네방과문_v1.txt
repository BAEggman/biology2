Draw another picture in exactly the same style as the one you just made - warm cream paper, thin hand-inked storybook outlines, soft flat watercolour washes, muted greys, tans and dusty blue-greens and brick reds, on bare cream ground, no text of any kind. Square image.

WHAT TO DRAW - one square building cut open at the front so you see inside, standing on bare cream ground. It is divided by one horizontal floor and one vertical wall into exactly FOUR rooms: TWO small rooms along the top and TWO large rooms below them. Draw the dividing wall and floor as thick solid masonry so the four rooms are unmistakable and easy to count.

THE WALLS: the LOWER LEFT room has walls drawn strikingly THICKER than every other room - a heavy massive wall, about three times the thickness of the lower right room's wall. The lower right room's wall is ordinary. The two small upper rooms have thin walls.

THE FOUR DOORS: in the floor between each upper room and the large room beneath it there is a hinged TRAPDOOR, and on the outer wall of each large room there is a hinged DOOR leading outside. That is four doors in total. Every one of the four doors is drawn hanging OPEN in one direction only, with a small wooden STOP BLOCK fixed behind it so it plainly cannot swing back the other way. Draw the stop blocks clearly.

WHAT IS INSIDE: the two upper rooms are small and nearly empty. The two lower rooms are large. Nothing else - no people, no furniture, no machines.

WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN: the building having any number of rooms other than four, or the dividing wall and floor being drawn so faintly the four rooms cannot be counted; the upper rooms being drawn larger than the lower ones - the upper two are clearly SMALLER; the lower left wall not being obviously thicker than the lower right wall - that difference is the whole point; any number of doors other than four; any door lacking its stop block, or a door drawn as a double swinging door that opens both ways; any person, animal, furniture, pipe, tube, machine, heart shape or blood appearing; any writing, letters, numbers, labels or arrows appearing anywhere.
