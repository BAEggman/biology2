Draw another picture in exactly the same style as the one you just made - warm cream paper, thin hand-inked storybook outlines, soft flat watercolour washes, on bare cream ground, no text of any kind. Square image. Keep it simple with plenty of empty cream paper.

WHAT TO DRAW - one tall glass TEST TUBE standing upright in a small wooden rack in the middle of the page, drawn large and clear. The tube has been left to settle, so its contents have separated into exactly THREE layers with sharp flat boundaries between them:

THE BOTTOM LAYER - a deep brick-red layer filling a little under half the tube.
THE MIDDLE LAYER - a very thin pale cream-white band, only a sliver, sitting right on top of the red.
THE TOP LAYER - a clear pale straw-yellow liquid filling a little over half the tube, plainly the tallest of the three.

Along the side of the glass draw plain unnumbered graduation TICKS, small horizontal marks like a measuring cylinder, so the heights of the layers can be compared. No numbers on them.

TO THE RIGHT of the rack, standing on the ground, draw ONE large single red DISC seen at a three-quarter angle - a rounded disc that is plainly DENTED INWARD in the centre on BOTH faces, like a squashed cushion or a doughnut whose hole never opened through. Its middle is thin and its rim is thick. It is completely plain inside - no core, no dot, no seed, nothing drawn in its centre.

Nothing else is in the picture.

WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN: any number of layers other than three; the layers being drawn in the wrong order or the wrong sizes - red at the bottom just under half, a thin white sliver in the middle, straw-yellow on top just over half and tallest; the middle white band being drawn thick - it is a sliver; the graduation ticks carrying numbers; the red disc being drawn as a ball, a coin, a wheel, a doughnut with a hole right through, or a cell with a nucleus, dot or core drawn inside it - it is dented on both faces and utterly plain in the middle; more than one red disc; any person, animal, microscope, syringe, label or background scenery; any writing, letters, numbers, labels or arrows appearing anywhere.
