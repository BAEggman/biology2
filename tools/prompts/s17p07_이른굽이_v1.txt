Draw another picture in exactly the same style - warm cream paper, thin hand-inked storybook outlines, soft flat watercolour washes, on bare cream ground, no text of any kind. Wide image, about twice as wide as it is tall. Keep it simple and uncluttered.

WHAT TO DRAW - one long pipe running left to right across the picture, made of two plainly different halves joined in the middle.

THE LEFT HALF is a fat ROUND pipe, smooth and cylindrical. Low down in its wall is an open hatch, and a wooden MATCHBOX with loose matchsticks spilling from it is sliding out through that hatch and falling away below the pipe, onto the ground. A cord is tied to the hatch flap and the flap is swinging shut.

THE RIGHT HALF is a SQUARE-SIDED duct that makes one sharp bend just after the join. Standing against the outside of this square duct is a wicker BASKET full of grey PEBBLES, and a rope tied to the basket's handle runs upward and is lifting the basket up the side of the duct. The basket is clearly rising.

ABOVE THE MIDDLE, hanging in the air over the join between the two halves, is a small square BOX with exactly FOUR round BUTTONS on its front face. Two cords come out of this box: one runs down to the left and is the cord tied to the hatch flap; the other runs down to the right and is the rope lifting the pebble basket.

ON THE SIDE OF THE SQUARE DUCT, past the bend, there is a small DOORWAY. Walking in through that doorway, side by side, are exactly two things: one plump SACK of white salt, and one GOAT. Fastened across that doorway is a bright GOLD PADLOCK, and hanging from the padlock's shackle by a short string is a small square TEA BAG with its paper tab.

Nothing else is in the picture. No people, no hands.

WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN: the two halves drawn the same shape - the left must be plainly round and the left half must never be square, and the right must be plainly square-sided; the matchbox drawn going INTO the pipe instead of falling out and away; the pebble basket drawn being lowered instead of raised, or sitting on the ground; the pebbles drawn as white salt or as coloured beads - they are plain grey stones; the box above having any number of buttons other than four; the two cords crossing each other or swapping sides; more than one sack or more than one goat, or a second animal of any kind; the padlock drawn in any colour but gold; the tea bag drawn hanging from anything other than the padlock; the gold padlock placed on the round left half; any writing, letters, numbers, labels or arrows appearing anywhere.
