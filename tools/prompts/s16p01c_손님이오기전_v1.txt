Draw another picture in exactly the same style as the one you just made - warm cream paper, thin hand-inked storybook outlines, soft flat watercolour washes, on bare cream ground, no text of any kind. Square image. Keep it simple and uncluttered.

WHAT TO DRAW - a long plain office WALL running across the picture, with service windows set into it, and a queue of customers standing in front of it. It is before opening: nobody is being served.

THE WALL: set into it are SEVEN service windows in a row. FIVE of them are bricked up solid - their openings are filled flush with red brick. ONE of them is a normal window with a wooden SHUTTER, and that shutter is CLOSED and has a small padlock on it. ONE of them is a WIDE window, twice as wide as the others, with room for two people to stand at it; it is empty.

ONE of the bricked-up windows has a wooden frame that is visibly TWISTED and warped out of square, unlike all the other frames which are straight. A customer's hand rests on that twisted frame.

THE QUEUE, four customers standing in front of the wall, each carrying one thing and nothing else:
- one standing at the SHUTTERED window, holding an OPEN CIGARETTE PACKET;
- one standing at the TWISTED bricked-up window, holding a small PULLEY with a rope running over it - it is this customer's hand that rests on the twisted frame;
- one standing at another bricked-up window, carrying a small metal DRUM whose lid has burst off and is flying up;
- one standing at the WIDE window, carrying a young potted SAPLING with a few leaves.

AT THE RIGHT END, on the ground away from the wall: a MAN sitting slumped on the floor with his legs out, resting, holding a flat two-sided TOKEN face down on his knee - he has turned it back over and stopped running.

Nothing else is in the picture. No clerks behind any window, no furniture.

WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN: any window standing open, or any clerk appearing behind a window; the number of bricked-up windows being fewer than the number of other windows - the bricked-up ones must plainly outnumber the rest; the shuttered window drawn without its closed shutter and padlock; more than one frame drawn twisted - exactly one, and a hand must rest on it; the wide window not being clearly wider than the others; any customer carrying more than one thing, or two customers carrying the same thing; the sitting man running or holding the token up in the air - he must be seated and the token must lie flat and face down; any writing, letters, numbers, labels or arrows appearing anywhere.
