One square illustration in a hand-inked storybook style: thin dark brown-black ink outlines, soft flat watercolour washes, a warm cream paper background, muted colours. No written words and no numbers anywhere in the picture.

the scene

A plain room. The left third of the picture is a cutaway showing the outside face of the room's wall. The rest is the room seen from inside, straight on, at eye level. Pale walls, a pale grey floor, teal-green ducting running along the ceiling.

what is in it, listed from left to right

1. far left, on the outside face of the wall. One teal-green metal switch box, mounted flat on the wall. Sticking out from its left side is one round D-shaped handle, like a capital D lying on its back. Nothing at all comes out of the right side of the box - that side is plain and bare.

2. the wall between that switch box and the door. Completely empty. There is no rod, no bar, no wire, no chain and no linkage of any kind between the switch box and the door - only plain empty wall. Nothing reaches from one to the other and nothing passes in front of or behind them.

3. below the switch box. One iron water valve with a round spoked wheel, turned wide open, feeding a short pipe that comes through the wall and ends in an open mouth just inside the room. One single fat water drop is falling from that pipe mouth, and on the floor directly below it one small splash of water has landed, with a few tiny droplets flying up out of the splash.

4. middle of the picture. One tall narrow red wooden door set into the wall, standing very slightly ajar. Hanging in the centre of that door is one large brass door knocker in the shape of a lion's head with a ring held in its mouth. The lion's mane and face are clearly drawn and the knocker is about as wide as a man's head. The lion-head knocker is the biggest single object on the door and it is easy to see at a glance that it is a lion.

5. right, filling the room's back wall. One large plain framed chart. On it one grey line rises steeply from the left, then runs perfectly flat and level for a long stretch across the middle, then drops steeply down at the right. The flat middle stretch is much longer than either slope.

what must not be in the picture

writing, letters or numbers; any rod, bar, wire, chain, pipe, shelf, picture frame or shadow that bridges the gap between the switch box and the door; a second door; a second lion; any animal head other than the one on the door; a dry, cracked or closed pipe - this pipe is open and running; a chart whose middle is a peak or a curve instead of a long flat level stretch; people.

what must be true when you are done

- there is exactly one switch box, one D-shaped handle, one door, one lion-head knocker, one open valve, one falling drop, one splash and one chart
- the wall between the switch box and the door is completely empty - nothing joins them, nothing touches either of them, and there is clear plain wall between
- the lion-head knocker sits on the door and is large enough to read clearly as a lion's face
- the valve is open and there is visible water: one drop in the air and one splash on the floor
- on the chart the flat middle stretch is clearly the longest part of the line
- one room, one square scene, not a grid and not split into panels
- warm cream paper background, thin hand-drawn linework, no lettering of any kind
