Draw another picture in exactly the same style as the one you just made - warm cream paper, thin hand-inked storybook outlines, soft flat watercolour washes, objects on bare cream ground, no text of any kind. Square image. Keep it uncluttered with plenty of empty cream paper.

WHAT TO DRAW - in the middle of the page, a cluster of small FUNNELS of different colours - some orange, some yellow, some dusty blue-green - all tilted inward so their spouts feed into ONE bigger funnel below them, like a bunch of flowers pouring into a single stem. Under the mouth of that one big funnel sit exactly TWO identical GREEN LEAVES, side by side and touching, drawn plainly with a midrib. From between those two leaves a single small pale BALL is springing upward and is just landing in a small shallow BOWL held up on a thin stand right above them.

AT THE UPPER LEFT: three or four straight coloured light-rays slant down toward the funnels from off the page - draw them as plain thin double lines in red, orange, blue and violet. One extra ray is GREEN, and that green ray alone does NOT reach the funnels: it strikes and BOUNCES AWAY back up to the left at an angle, drawn with a clear kink where it bounces.

AT THE RIGHT: one small ORANGE PARASOL, open, held on a thin pole planted in the ground, its canopy tilted to shade the funnel cluster from the harshest ray.

Nothing else is in the picture. No people, no plants other than the two leaves, no buildings.

WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN: more or fewer than two green leaves under the big funnel, or the two leaves being drawn different from each other - they are identical twins side by side; the green ray reaching the funnels or being absorbed - it must clearly bounce off and go back up; the other coloured rays bouncing - only the green one bounces; the funnels being drawn as flowers, trumpets, horns, cones of light or umbrellas - they are plain kitchen funnels with a wide mouth and a narrow spout; the small funnels all being the same colour - they must be visibly different colours; the parasol being drawn as an umbrella with a handle held by a person - nobody holds it, it stands on its own pole; the bowl being drawn as a cup, hat, dish of food or lamp - it is a plain shallow bowl on a thin stand; any sun, cloud, sky, ground line, building, room or background scenery; any writing, letters, numbers, labels or arrows appearing anywhere.
