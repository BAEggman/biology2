the scene

One plain grey wooden shelf unit seen straight on, filling the whole picture. It has five open compartments stacked one above the other — count them: five, each with its own shelf board and its own clear gap of empty space. A single pale blue cord runs down through all five compartments, so it reads as one continuous cord from top to bottom.

what is in each compartment, from the top down

1. the blue cord lies loose in slack waves, wound around nothing.

2. one wooden barrel lying on its side, with the blue cord wrapped twice around its middle — two bands of cord, no more. The barrel's body is four upright staves of four different colours, side by side, and each stave carries one thing and nothing else:
- the pale cream stave: one small flat brass step-ladder — two upright legs with exactly two flat rungs between them. It has no knobs, no balls and no round parts at all.
- the warm terracotta stave: one small flat brass clasp — a straight upright bar with exactly two round bellies bulging out from it, one belly above the other, so the whole clasp looks like a letter with two round bumps. Nothing else on this stave.
- the dull olive stave: exactly three round brass studs in a level row across the middle, and nothing else.
- the soft blue-grey stave: exactly four round brass studs in a level row across the middle, and nothing else.
Just below the barrel, gripping the cord where it leaves the barrel, is one small iron clip, quite separate from the barrel, carrying exactly one round brass stud.

3. six identical small barrels threaded along the blue cord like beads, evenly spaced; at the right-hand end the cord twists up into a thicker rope.

4. that rope is slung over a horizontal wooden rail and hangs below it in four large open loops — count them: four.

5. the rope is wound into one short thick coil sitting on the bottom shelf, alone in its own compartment.
