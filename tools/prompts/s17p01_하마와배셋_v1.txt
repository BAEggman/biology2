One square illustration in a hand-inked storybook style: thin dark brown-black ink outlines, soft flat watercolour washes, a warm cream paper background, muted colours. No words, letters or numbers anywhere. / THE SETTING: A quayside seen from slightly above. Along the top runs a row of market stalls with striped awnings and crates of produce, with two plain wooden DOORS in the wall at the upper right. Below the stalls is a stone quay, and below that a wide green-blue RIVER filling the lower half of the picture. / 1. THE MAN STANDING ASTRIDE, LOWER MIDDLE: Two pale wooden mooring POSTS stand up out of the water side by side with a narrow gap between them. One man stands on top of them with one boot on each post, legs spread wide apart, hands on hips, so the only way past him is the NARROW GAP between his straddled legs. He is the only figure standing on posts. / 2. THE SPIKED NET AND THE BUNDLE, LOWER LEFT: A dark SPIKED NET fence is strung between more posts to his left. A single round straw-wrapped BUNDLE floats just outside that fence and a bold ARROW beside it points AWAY from the fence, so the bundle is plainly being pushed back and cannot get through. / 3. THE TWO PORTERS ON THE QUAY - COUNT THE SACKS: Two porters walk along the stone quay, well apart from each other and drawn large enough to compare. (A) THE FIRST PORTER carries ONE plump sack over his shoulder and ONE small square parcel under his other arm - one sack, one parcel, held loosely. (B) THE SECOND PORTER carries TWO plump sacks, one on each shoulder, and ONE small square parcel that he CLUTCHES tightly against his chest with both forearms, gripping it hard - two sacks, one parcel, held tight. Never give either man a different number of sacks. / 4. THE PARCEL LEAVING BY THE BACK DOOR: At the upper right a third man carries one small square parcel out through one of the plain wooden doors in the back wall, his back to us, half through the doorway. / 5. THE THREE BOATS - THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT PART: On the river at the right float EXACTLY THREE wooden boats and no more, spaced apart in a row so all three are seen at once and easily compared. They must be plainly different: (i) THE PASSING BOAT is loaded with a neat stack of crates and rides normally; nothing is being taken off or put on it. (ii) THE EMPTIED BOAT is completely EMPTY - its bare planked floor and inner ribs are clearly visible, no crates at all - and it rides HIGH out of the water. (iii) THE OVERLOADED BOAT is piled with far more crates than the others, heaped above its sides, and it rides LOW so its gunwale is almost level with the water. / 6. THE HIPPO - THE ONE NEW THING: Standing on the deck of the OVERLOADED boat, on top of and among its crates, is one large grey HIPPOPOTAMUS - a fat barrel-bodied animal with a huge blunt square muzzle, tiny ears and short stumpy legs, unmistakably a hippo. It is the only animal in the whole picture. Its weight is what pushes that boat down so its side nearly dips under. The other two boats carry no animal. / WHAT MUST NOT BE IN THE PICTURE: a fourth boat; an octopus; letters, numbers or writing of any kind; any animal other than the one hippo; a porter with three sacks. / WHAT MUST BE TRUE WHEN YOU ARE DONE: one man stands astride two posts with a narrow gap between his legs. A spiked net pushes one round bundle away, with an arrow pointing away from the fence. One porter has one sack and one loose parcel; the other has two sacks and clutches his parcel tight. One parcel is going out through a back door. Exactly three boats: one loaded and riding normally, one completely empty and riding high, one overloaded and riding low with a large hippopotamus standing on its cargo.
