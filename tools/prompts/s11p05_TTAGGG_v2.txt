One square illustration in a hand-inked storybook style: thin dark brown-black ink outlines, soft flat watercolour washes, a warm cream paper background, muted colours. The ONLY letters anywhere in the picture are the six branded letters described in part 3; nothing else carries writing or numbers. / THE SETTING: Three railway tracks lie one above another, running left to right across the picture like three shelves - a TOP track, a MIDDLE track and a BOTTOM track. All three are the same track drawn three times and must look identical in their left-hand three quarters. / 1. THE LEFT THREE QUARTERS OF EVERY TRACK: wooden SLEEPERS of clearly DIFFERENT lengths, no two the same, laid in exactly the same order on all three tracks. This part must be identical on all three - not one sleeper missing or shortened anywhere. / 2. THE RIGHT END OF EVERY TRACK: a run of SHORT SLEEPERS that are all exactly the same length as one another, laid in a repeating row. The TOP track has a long run of these repeats. The BOTTOM track has the same long run. The MIDDLE track has only TWO of them left, so its right end is plainly shorter than the other two - and yet its left three quarters is untouched and just as long as theirs. / 3. THE BRANDED SLEEPERS - THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT PART, AND THE LETTERS COME IN TWO GROUPS OF THREE: Every one of those short repeating sleepers is branded with letters running down its length, and they are laid out as TWO SEPARATE GROUPS OF THREE with a clear EMPTY GAP between the groups, so each group is easy to count on its own. The UPPER group, nearer the outer end of the track, is THREE letters: T, then T, then A. The LOWER group, below the gap, is THREE letters and every one of them is the same letter G: G, then G, then G - three G's, count them, not two. So each sleeper reads as a group of three (T T A), a gap, then a group of three (G G G). Draw the letters LARGE, dark and well spaced, like letters burnt into wood, and make these short sleepers WIDE enough and TALL enough that both groups of three sit comfortably with the gap between them. Every short sleeper carries the same two groups in the same order. The long uneven sleepers on the left carry no letters at all. / 4. THE MAN WITH THE STEEL BAND: At the right end of the BOTTOM track stands one workman with a wide SILVER STEEL BAND slung diagonally across his back like a sash. He has laid the free end of that steel band flat against the end of the track, in line with the rails, and is setting a fresh WOODEN sleeper down on top of the band so the track grows longer. The band is bright metal; the sleepers are plain wood; that difference in material must be obvious. / 5. THE EMPTY TRACKS: No person stands at the right end of the TOP track or the MIDDLE track. Only the bottom track has a workman. / WHAT MUST NOT BE IN THE PICTURE: any writing other than the six branded letters; numbers; a fourth track; a workman on the top or middle track. / WHAT MUST BE TRUE WHEN YOU ARE DONE: three tracks whose left three quarters are identical uneven sleepers; identical short repeating sleepers at the right end of all three, but only TWO left on the middle one; every short sleeper branded with a group of three (T T A), a gap, then a group of three G's (G G G) down its length, large and legible; one workman at the bottom right with a silver steel band across his back, laying the band against the track end and setting a wooden sleeper on it; nobody at the other two track ends.
