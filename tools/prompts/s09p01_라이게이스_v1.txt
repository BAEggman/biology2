One square illustration in a hand-inked storybook style: thin dark brown-black ink outlines, soft flat watercolour washes, a warm cream paper background, muted colours. No written words and no numbers anywhere in the picture.

the scene

One long wooden workbench running diagonally across the picture from lower left to upper right. Lying along the bench is one long flat teal strip like a ribbon of tape. Two workmen stand behind the bench.

what is on the strip and the bench

1. printed on the teal strip is a row of plain marks — a left-pointing arrow, a small diamond, a cross — and then the same three marks again in the opposite order and facing the other way, so the row reads the same from either end, mirrored about its middle.

2. left of centre, one workman in a green shirt and mustard apron holds one pair of large open shears and is cutting the strip.

3. lying loose on the bench in front, two short cut pieces of the same strip:
- the nearer piece has been cut on a slant so that a stepped tongue sticks out at each end, and the tongue of one piece fits into the notch of the other
- the further piece has been cut straight across, so both its ends are flat and square with nothing sticking out

4. right of centre, one workman in a grey shirt and rust-red apron leans over a joint in the strip. In his RIGHT hand he holds a soldering iron with a fine tip, pressed to the joint, and a row of small solder beads runs along the seam. In his LEFT hand, held up clearly away from his body, he holds one small metal CIGARETTE LIGHTER with its lid flipped open and one small yellow flame standing up from it.

what must be true when you are done

- the row of marks on the strip is mirrored about its centre — the same three shapes on each side, facing opposite ways
- the shears are open and biting into the strip
- one loose piece has slanted interlocking tongues; the other has flat square ends with no tongue at all, and the difference is easy to see
- the second workman holds the soldering iron in one hand and the lit lighter in the other, and the lighter's flame is clearly burning
- the lighter is unmistakably a small metal pocket lighter with a hinged lid, not a torch, not a candle and not a match
- one bench, one square scene, not a grid and not split into panels
- warm cream paper background, thin hand-drawn linework, no lettering of any kind
