Work only from the attached picture. Redraw the attached picture keeping everything exactly as it is, and add ONE new thing. THE ADDITION - a SIXTH PERSON, a man who was turned away at the door. Put him in the wide EMPTY FLOOR in the lower right quarter of the picture, below and to the left of the open doorway, standing well clear of everyone else and touching nobody. He has just come away from the door and is walking back towards the viewer, and three things about him are meant to be noticed. ONE - HIS HANDS ARE EMPTY: both hands are open and empty, palms turned up, holding no envelope, no letter and no paper of any kind - that is why he could not get through the door. TWO - HE CANNOT KEEP HIS BALANCE: he is caught mid-stagger, his legs crossed and tangled under him as if the floor were tilting, one arm flung out sideways for balance and his upper body tipped off its axis, like someone on a rolling boat. THREE - HE DOES NOT NOTICE WHAT IS UNDER HIS FEET: he is BAREFOOT and he is stepping into a loose scatter of small DRAWING PINS spilled across the floor around his feet, without looking down and with a completely calm, untroubled face - he simply pays them no attention. Draw the pins as many small scattered tacks with round flat heads, too many to count, and do not arrange them in a row or a pattern. Colour him differently from the others: his face and his bare hands are painted a pale washed-out cream, clearly lighter than the warm skin of everyone else in the picture, while his clothes are ordinary muted greens and browns like theirs. His nose is ORDINARY SKIN COLOUR - it is NOT blue. He is an ordinary sized man, the same height as the other adults. Nothing else is added. Everything else stays exactly identical, and the small counted things matter most - do not add, remove, move or re-count a single one of them: the seated man at the lower left with his NECKTIE, his ankles bound by rope, the stones by his bare feet and the small red berries by his hand and on the floor, all exactly as many as now; the girl with the two YELLOW RIBBONS in her hair and the yellow patches at the corners of her mouth, exactly as many as now; the white-haired old man with the SHOES hung on a strap around his neck, exactly as many shoes as now, his empty metal bucket, his blank stare and his torn sleeve; the woman carrying the baby in the sling with its unclosed zip, holding up the fan of LEAVES with exactly as many leaves as now, not one more and not one fewer; the man with the COBALT BLUE NOSE at the doorway, still holding out his white ENVELOPE and still stepping through the open door; the open wooden door and its frame; the long curving wall and the plain floor; the same warm cream paper, the same thin hand-inked storybook linework and soft flat watercolour washes. WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN: any change to the number of leaves, ribbons, yellow patches, shoes, berries or stones; the new man carrying an envelope, a letter, a bag, a bucket or anything at all; the new man having a blue nose, or the blue-nosed man losing his; a second new person appearing, or the new man standing on the doorstep or blocking the doorway; the drawing pins being drawn as nails, screws, stones, coins or berries, or laid out in a line; the new man sitting or lying down - he must be upright and mid-stumble; his face showing distress or a grimace - it must be calm; his ankles being tied with rope; the blue-nosed man being moved, stopped or turned around; the old man's bucket filling up; any writing, letters or numbers appearing anywhere.
